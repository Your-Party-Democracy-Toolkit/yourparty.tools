  #title
  Pretending it's a formal branch, when the national party doesn't exist yet
  
  #content
    ::::div{.accordion-content-div}
    There is no membership and no national forms yet... so how do you pretend it's a formal branch? You would rightly criticise permanent structures at the national level before founding conference. It is a bit presumptuous and pre-emptive?
    
    Instead, use this as a time for experimentation. Practice the democracy you want to see across the party right now, including the use of temporary organising committees and things like that.
    
    Goals: political education that links key debates and political perspectives with practices of democracy, grounded in your local constituents' issues.
    ::::
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Engaging with undemocratic structures
  
  #content
  Act with honesty and transparency with an intent for establishing proto-democracy for YP. Consider a stepped approach, whilst understanding where your boundaries are at each stage.
  
  1. Engage in good faith - develop tactics to put democracy on meeting agendas which could involve engaging with any established or self-appointed committee and/or making interventions in branch meetings. The [constitution](/organising/infrastructure/constitution) guide includes examples of structures and motions.
  2. Advocate for transparency on political affiliations - these should be declared when a person speaks in a meeting and included on the name when in online meetings.
  3. Form a caucus within the undemocratic structure - creating opportunities to mobilise supporters and practice better democracy that you demand of the main group
  4. Form a faction - a political focus of comrades that stand for democracy, where you set up a Proto-Branch as a faction that reflects the values you wish to practice. No YP group is more, nor less sovereign than the next group.
  
  Read the guide on factions, caucuses and fractions \[link needed]
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Dealing with transphobia (and other forms of oppression)
  
  #content
  Open group chats and meetings can sometimes attract individuals whose interventions undermine inclusive discussion. In recent years, this has included people advancing transphobic arguments, often emboldened by court rulings and political shifts. Some of these individuals present as legally savvy and well-resourced, so while this note is not legal advice, the following points may help in managing such situations.
  
  It’s important to distinguish between holding a belief and imposing it on others. Employment Tribunal decisions have confirmed that “Gender Critical” views amount to a protected philosophical belief under the Equality Act 2010. Excluding someone simply because they hold those views could be discriminatory. The line is crossed when these beliefs are used to deny the existence or rights of others, or to dominate discussion in ways that exclude, harass, or silence fellow participants.
  
  **Focus on behaviours, not beliefs. Consider:**
  
  - Are they participating in good-faith discussion?
  - Are their contributions relevant to the topic at hand?
  - Are they using the space to promote transphobia?
  - Are they engaging in harassing or demeaning behaviour?
  - Are they denying the existence, dignity, or rights of others in the group?
  - Are they leveraging an existing platform or authority to impose their views?
  - Have complaints been raised about them?
  - Are they preventing constructive discussion from taking place?
  
  **Possible graduated responses include:**
  
  - Having a one-to-one conversation to explore their perspective and make clear the group’s expectations.
  - Temporarily muting or removing them from the chat as a “time out”.
  - Permanently removing them if behaviour does not improve.
  
  Whatever approach you take, ensure there is a clear rationale, keep records, and make decisions collectively wherever possible. Emphasise that this is about managing the health of a group chat or meeting for an organisation that doesn't exist yet. You're not making judgements on party membership or wider participation in political activity.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Focussing minds on the key national debates
  
  #content
  Members will have signed up to YP for various reasons and with different motivations. Some may not be interested in the details of the key national debates, but are more motivated by specific policies.
  
  Within a democratic structure, it's not always realistic to get every key debate on the meeting agenda. A key aim at this early stage should be to build a collective awareness within your group. Through this process, you can steer discussions toward the key debates.
  
  For example, if members create policy ideas about the NHS, the discussion could pivot toward how these ideas are submitted to the national party. This could lead to a discussion about how democracy and conferences function.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Understanding the position of already established groups
  
  #content
  There are many national and local political , community and trade union groups that exist within our movement. Each will have varying positions and democratic structures for how they're engaging with YP at these early stages.
  
  One of the key [national debates](/organising/proto-branch/key-debates) is how the party is structured and one of these options is a federal or 'umbrella' model where groups affiliate to YP. This might mean that an affiliated group confers affiliate membership to YP for their members, that the affiliate becomes entitled to block votes on decision making bodies and an affiliate stands for election under a YP banner.
  
  Variations of this model potentially allows established groups to gain significant influence and power within YP and members of these groups may provide resistance to attempts to create Proto-Branches through mass member democracy and for debates on the national party structure and democracy. You'll likely encounter arguments or actions that attempt to deflect discussions for mass member democracy as follows:
  
  - It's too early to start thinking about structures and electing a committee, we should be prioritising being more representative and reaching out to communities first so that any future election is as representative as possible.
  - Advocating for a "workers' party" rather than a "socialist party" is a subtle distinction that usually means the involvement and affiliations of trade unions. It's important to seek clarity on if that means trade unions as bureaucratic organisations, or workers on the ground that are in trade unions.
  - Actions that appear to support setting up democratic structures, but that set out multi-month timelines for moving towards an elected committee.
  
  New activists will be getting involved who may be new to politics and it's important that they understand the meaning behind some of these arguments or actions that superficially sound reasonable. Act with honesty, transparency, and with a clear intent in your arguments and robustly challenge when other speakers are not.
  
  A key counter argument is that through experimenting with Proto-Branch structures, members are practicing and experiencing democracy. That can only be a good thing and provides real-life experience and political education on learning what good democracy looks and feels like - perfect for forming a view on democracy in the national party! It's also important to emphasise two concepts that may not be familiar:
  
  - Proto-Branch Committees should exist for short periods, dissolve and be re-elected regularly every few weeks. This allow space for reflection, improvement to structures and widens participation as more members join.
  - Members of Proto-Branch Committees should recuse from standing in any formal constituted YP structures for at least 1 year. This prevents establishing power structures and ensures that members of the Proto-Branch committees are focussed on these early stages, and not in establishing themselves as a long term careerist in the branch.

  - On the question of representation and outreach. And how to do it.
